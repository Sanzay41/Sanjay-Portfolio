Reservoir Site Suitability, Tehrathum District

GIS analysis for an M.Sc. thesis that identifies where water reservoirs can be built safely in Nepal’s mid-hills.

Thesis
Site suitability analysis for construction of water reservoirs in Tehrathum District and Laligurans Municipality using geospatial techniques

Author

Background and problem

Tehrathum lies in Nepal’s mid-hills, with elevations from 169 m to 3,038 m. Heavy monsoon rainfall runs off steep slopes before it can be stored, and many traditional springs have dried up, leaving communities short of water in the dry season. Small and medium reservoirs can capture monsoon runoff, but in this terrain a poorly chosen site risks slope failure, seepage and wasted public budgets.

Objectives

  1. Prepare eight criterion layers covering terrain, rainfall, land cover, vegetation, soil and proximity.
  2. Weight the criteria with the Analytic Hierarchy Process.
  3. Combine them by weighted overlay into low, medium and high suitability zones.
  4. Report suitable area for each of the district’s six local governments.

Workflow

  1. ProblemWhere can reservoirs be built safely and cost-effectively?
  2. Data30 m DEM, DoHM rainfall, Sentinel-2, SOTER soil, roads, rivers, settlements
  3. MethodGIS multi-criteria analysis with AHP weights
  4. ProcessingEight layers projected to UTM 45N and reclassified 1–5
  5. Results8.51% of the analysed area highly suitable
  6. ImplicationsPrioritise municipal reservoir budgets for high-suitability land

Study area and criteria

Every layer was projected to WGS 1984 UTM Zone 45N, converted to raster where needed and reclassified to a common 1–5 suitability scale.

Results

Slope and rainfall carried the highest AHP weights, followed by land cover and NDVI; soil and the three distance criteria carried the least. The consistency ratio was below the 0.10 threshold.

5,648 haHigh suitability, 8.51% of the analysed area
57,566 haMedium suitability, 86.74%
3,155 haLow suitability, 4.75%
231 haHigh suitability in Laligurans Municipality, 2.6% of its area

High-suitability land is scattered in small pockets. Phedap has the largest share of its area in the high class (12.9%) and Laligurans the smallest (2.6%), which matters for a municipality looking to place reservoirs within its own boundary.

Suitability is a screening result

High suitability identifies where to look first. Each candidate site still needs field investigation of geology, seepage, land ownership and catchment yield before design.

My contribution

  • Acquired and pre-processed the spatial datasets and projected them to a common coordinate system.
  • Produced the eight criterion layers, including slope, interpolated rainfall, land cover, NDVI and distance surfaces.
  • Reclassified the layers to the 1–5 scale and ran the weighted overlay with the AHP weights.
  • Calculated suitability areas for each local government and prepared the thesis map sheets.
  • The research design, AHP judgements and interpretation are the author’s.

Limitations and next steps

  • Resolution. A 30 m DEM can miss the small depressions that make a site viable in steep terrain.
  • Subsurface. Without detailed geological data, the model assumes uniform ground stability.
  • Weighting. AHP weights rest on expert judgement; other experts could produce a different map.
  • Validation. No validation against existing reservoir sites (such as ROC analysis) is reported yet.
