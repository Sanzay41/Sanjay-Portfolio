A modest town that needs direction, not capacity.

Business-as-usual land-cover projection and land suitability analysis supporting the structure plan for Rakam Karnali New Town.

  1. ProblemDirect modest growth onto safe, serviceable land
  2. Data2015 land cover, roads, settlements, infrastructure, rivers, forest, slope, elevation
  3. MethodologyCA–Markov BAU to 2055; AHP weighted overlay
  4. GIS processingSeven criteria reclassified 1–5, then forest, river and hazard masks
  5. ResultsBuilt-up 63 to 251 ha; 691 ha high or very high after masking
  6. Planning implicationsCompare trend with capability to guide growth boundary and phasing

The planning problem. If current trends simply continue, how much land would the town consume, where, and does that growth land on safe, serviceable ground? The projection shows the trend. The suitability analysis shows the capability. Read together they point to where a modest amount of growth should be directed.

1 · Trend

Business-as-usual projection, 2015 to 2055

Select a year to see the map and class areas.

    Scenario output, not a forecast. Only 2015 is a baseline map; 2025 onward are projections.

    What it shows

    Built-up land grows from 63 ha to 251 ha, an average of about 4.7 ha a year, and occupies only 4.6% of the area by 2055. Agriculture falls from 1,697 ha to 1,510 ha, so almost all new built-up land comes from farmland. Forest stays near 62% and water near 5%. On the maps, growth lines the road corridors and a river-side strip.

    What I would refine next

    The 2015 baseline is eleven years old and 2025 is itself a projection. The next step is to classify 2025 imagery, compare it with the projection and recalibrate, and to report a validation statistic such as Kappa.

    2 · Capability

    Which land is suitable for urban development?

    Seven criteria, grouped as accessibility, physical capability and environmental safety, combined by AHP weights.

    CriterionAHP weight
    Road18%
    Settlement15%
    Infrastructure16%
    River15%
    Forest10%
    Slope16%
    Elevation10%
    Total100%

    Each criterion is reclassified to a five-class scale. The weighted overlay ranks every cell from very low to very high suitability, then forest, river and hazard land are masked out so that growth is never directed to land that cannot be developed.

    Planning implications

    Use the trend as a benchmark, not a forecast

    • Growth is modest, so it can be accommodated safely. What the trend lacks is direction.
    • Concentrate growth in nodes on the most suitable land instead of letting it string out along roads.
    • Keep growth off the river-side strip where the trend and the hazard overlap.
    • Protect farmland outside the urban growth boundary.
