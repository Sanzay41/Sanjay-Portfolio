Rakam Karnali New Town

Business-as-usual land-cover projection and land suitability analysis supporting the structure plan for a small new town in Dailekh District.

Location
Aathbis Municipality, Dailekh District, Karnali Province
Client
New Town Project Coordination Office, DUDBC
Assignment
Review and update of the Integrated Development Plan, and preparation of the Structure Plan and Land Use Plan
Consultant credited on map sheets
Sitara Consult Pvt. Ltd.
My role
GIS analyst (through Picasso Engineering Consultancy): business-as-usual land-cover projection and land suitability analysis
Software
TerrSet (CA–Markov); ArcGIS Pro (AHP weighted overlay)
Study area
5,488 ha for the projection, held constant across all years

Background and problem

Rakam Karnali is a small new town on the Madhya Pahadi Lokmarga (Mid-Hill Highway). If current trends simply continue, how much land would the town consume, where would it go, and would that growth fall on safe, serviceable ground? The projection describes the trend and the suitability analysis describes the land's capability; read together, they show where modest growth should be directed.

Objectives

  1. Project land cover from the 2015 baseline to 2055 under business as usual.
  2. Rank land suitability with seven AHP-weighted criteria.
  3. Remove forest, river and hazard land as constraints.
  4. Compare the projected trend with land capability to inform the growth boundary and phasing.

Workflow

  1. ProblemDirect modest growth onto safe, serviceable land
  2. Data2015 land cover, roads, settlements, infrastructure, rivers, forest, slope, elevation
  3. MethodCA–Markov projection to 2055; AHP weighted overlay
  4. ProcessingSeven criteria reclassified 1–5, then forest, river and hazard masks
  5. ResultsBuilt-up land projected from 63 to 251 ha; 691 ha high or very high after masking
  6. ImplicationsGuide the growth boundary, nodes and phasing
Step 1

Business-as-usual projection, 2015 to 2055

Select a year to see the map and class areas.

    Only 2015 is an observed map. Every later year is a projection: a business-as-usual scenario, not a forecast.

    Observed baseline

    In 2015 the study area was mostly forest (3,419 ha) and agriculture (1,697 ha), with 63 ha of built-up land along the road corridors.

    Projected trend

    Under business as usual, built-up land grows to 251 ha by 2055, about 4.7 ha a year, and still covers only 4.6% of the area. Agriculture falls to 1,510 ha, so almost all new built-up land comes from farmland. Forest stays near 62% and water near 5%. On the maps, projected growth follows the road corridors and a riverside strip.

    Step 2

    Which land is suitable for urban development?

    Seven criteria, grouped as accessibility, physical capability and environmental safety, combined with AHP weights.

    CriterionAHP weight
    Road18%
    Infrastructure16%
    Slope16%
    Settlement15%
    River15%
    Forest10%
    Elevation10%
    Total100%

    Each criterion is reclassified to a five-class scale. The weighted overlay ranks every cell from very low to very high suitability. Forest, river and hazard land are then masked out, so growth is never directed to land that cannot be developed.

    After masking, 691 ha remains in the high and very high classes, far more than the 188 ha of additional built-up land projected by 2055.

    Study-area base maps

    Map sheets prepared as the spatial base for the structure plan.

    Planning implications

    Use the trend as a benchmark, not a forecast.

    • Growth is modest and can be accommodated safely. What the trend lacks is direction.
    • Concentrate growth in nodes on the most suitable land instead of letting it string out along roads.
    • Keep growth off the riverside strip, where the projected trend and the hazard overlap.
    • Protect farmland outside the urban growth boundary.

    My contribution

    • Set up and ran the business-as-usual CA–Markov projection in TerrSet from the 2015 baseline.
    • Prepared and reclassified the seven suitability criteria and applied the AHP weights.
    • Applied the natural-resource and hazard masks and produced the suitability map sheets.

    Limitations and next steps

    • Dated baseline. The 2015 baseline is eleven years old and 2025 is itself a projection. The next step is to classify 2025 imagery, compare it with the projection and recalibrate.
    • Validation. No agreement statistic (for example Kappa) has been reported yet.
    • Suitability is not developability. Tenure, ground conditions and detailed hazard checks are still needed at site level.
