Bheriganga Valley Model New Town

GIS multi-criteria land suitability analysis for the Detailed Master Plan and Detailed Project Report of a model new town in Surkhet District.

Location
Bheriganga, Gurbhakot and Lekbeshi municipalities, Surkhet District, Karnali Province
Study area
660.4 km²
Client
New Town Project Coordination Office, Department of Urban Development and Building Construction (DUDBC)
Organisation
Picasso Engineering Consultancy
My role
GIS analyst: data preparation, reclassification, AHP weighting and weighted overlay
Software
ArcGIS Pro
Assignment
Detailed Master Plan and Detailed Project Report for Bheriganga Valley Model New Town
Output
Land suitability map in five classes

Background and problem

A model new town needs land that is buildable, accessible and safe, and that avoids sensitive forest and dense existing settlement. Across three municipalities and about 660 km², those factors had to be combined into a single, defensible suitability map that the master-planning team could work from.

Objectives

  1. Assemble a consistent spatial database of terrain, forest, settlement, river and road layers.
  2. Weight nine criteria with the Analytic Hierarchy Process.
  3. Combine them by weighted overlay into a five-class land suitability map.
  4. Quantify the area in each suitability class to inform land-use and infrastructure planning.

Workflow

  1. ProblemWhich land can support a model new town?
  2. DataSRTM DEM, national land-use data, settlements, roads and rivers from imagery
  3. MethodMulti-criteria analysis with AHP weights
  4. ProcessingNine layers reclassified 1–5 and combined by weighted overlay
  5. ResultsHigh and very high suitability on about 58% of the study area
  6. ImplicationsStarting point for land-use planning and infrastructure allocation

Data sources

Each layer was converted to a raster with a common projection and cell size, then reclassified to a 1–5 scale.

LayerSourcePurpose
Elevation (DEM)SRTM 30 m, USGS EarthExplorerTerrain and elevation criteria
Slope and aspectDerived from the DEMConstruction feasibility; solar exposure
ForestDepartment of Survey, National Land Use ProjectEnvironmentally sensitive and restricted areas
Settlement, density and proximityDigitised from 2025 satellite imageryExisting settlement, displacement risk, expansion potential
River network and proximityDEM, imagery digitisation, National Land Use ProjectFlood susceptibility and water access
Road network and proximityImagery digitisation and National Land Use ProjectAccessibility and infrastructure cost

Method

Pairwise comparison matrices on Saaty’s 1–9 scale gave the relative importance of each criterion. The normalised principal eigenvector provided the weights, and the consistency ratio was kept below 0.10.

The suitability score is the weighted sum S = Σ(Wi × Xi), where Wi is the criterion weight and Xi its reclassified value. The score was then grouped into five suitability classes.

Result

About 383 km², roughly 58% of the study area, falls in the high and very high classes. The valley has a substantial base of favourable land for planned expansion, and the map gives planners a repeatable starting point for infrastructure allocation and growth management.

Suitability is not developability

A high score means a location performs well against the nine criteria at the scale of the analysis. It does not establish that the land is legally available, environmentally cleared or physically buildable. Tenure, protected-area rules, detailed geotechnical conditions and local hazards must be checked before land is allocated.

My contribution

  • Prepared and harmonised the nine input layers, including digitising settlements, roads and rivers from 2025 imagery.
  • Reclassified each criterion to the 1–5 scale.
  • Derived the AHP weights and checked consistency.
  • Ran the weighted overlay in ArcGIS Pro and produced the suitability map sheet and area table.

Limitations and next steps

  • Resolution. The 30 m SRTM DEM limits the detail of slope and elevation criteria in steep terrain.
  • Weights. The individual AHP weights are not yet published on this page; adding them would make the analysis fully reproducible.
  • Hazards. The suitability map should be read alongside flood and landslide hazard mapping before land allocation.
  • Field validation. High-suitability areas need site checks for tenure, ground conditions and access.
