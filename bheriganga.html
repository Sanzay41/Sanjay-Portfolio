Finding land for a model new town.

A GIS multi-criteria suitability analysis for the Detailed Master Plan and Detailed Project Report of Bheriganga Valley Model New Town.

  1. ProblemWhich land can support a model new town?
  2. DataSRTM DEM, national land-use data, settlements, roads and rivers from imagery
  3. MethodologyMulti-criteria analysis with AHP weights
  4. GIS processingNine layers reclassified 1–5 and combined by weighted overlay
  5. ResultsHigh and very high suitability on about 58% of the study area
  6. Planning implicationsBasis for land-use planning, infrastructure allocation and growth management
Location
Bheriganga, Gurbhakot and Lekbeshi municipalities, Surkhet District, Karnali Province
Client
New Town Project Coordination Office (map sheet credit)
Study area
About 660 km²
My role
GIS analysis: data preparation, reclassification, AHP weighting and weighted overlay
Tools
ArcGIS Pro; AHP; weighted overlay
Output
Land suitability map in five classes
The planning problem. A model new town needs land that is buildable, accessible and safe, and that avoids sensitive forest and dense existing settlement. Across three municipalities, that means combining many spatial factors into one defensible map.

Data

Nine layers, one common scale

Each layer is rasterised to a common projection and cell size, then reclassified to 1–5.

LayerSourcePurpose
DEM (elevation)USGS EarthExplorer, SRTM, 30 mTerrain and elevation criteria
Slope and aspectDerived from the DEMConstruction feasibility; solar exposure
ForestDepartment of Survey, National Land Use ProjectEnvironmentally sensitive and restricted areas
Settlement, settlement density, proximityManual digitisation of 2025 satellite imageryExisting settlement, displacement risk, expansion potential
River network and proximityDEM, imagery digitisation, National Land Use ProjectFlood susceptibility and water access
Road network and proximityImagery digitisation and National Land Use ProjectAccessibility and infrastructure cost

Method

AHP weights and weighted overlay

Relative importance from pairwise comparison, then a weighted sum.

Pairwise comparison matrices on Saaty’s 1–9 scale gave the relative importance of each criterion. The normalised eigenvector became the weight, and the consistency ratio was kept below 0.1.

The final score is the weighted sum S = Σ(Wi × Xi), where Wi is the weight and Xi the reclassified value, then classified into five suitability classes.

Result

High and very high suitability across 58% of the area

What it means. About 383 km² falls in the High and Very High classes, so the valley has a substantial base of favourable land for planned expansion. The map gives planners a defensible, repeatable starting point for infrastructure allocation and growth management.
