Land-Change Modelling with CA–Markov

Business-as-usual land-cover scenarios for Dharan and Rakam Karnali: how they are built, what they show and how far they can be trusted.

What a business-as-usual scenario is

A business-as-usual (BAU) scenario asks what happens if recent land-cover trends simply continue, with no new plan or enforcement. It is a benchmark to test a plan against, not a prediction of the future. Real growth will depend on policy, infrastructure, markets and hazards that the model does not represent.

I build these scenarios in TerrSet with a Cellular Automata–Markov (CA–Markov) model. The Markov chain decides how much land changes class in each step, from transition probabilities between two observed maps. The cellular automaton decides where that change goes, favouring cells that are suitable and next to existing cells of the same class.

Projects
Dharan (2004–2050) and Rakam Karnali (2015–2055); Chandannath Municipality (not documented on this site)
Software
ArcGIS Pro for classification and raster preparation; TerrSet for CA–Markov
Input imagery
Historical Google Earth imagery, classified into land-cover maps

Workflow

  1. Historical land coverClassify each date into one legend
  2. HarmoniseOne grid, one extent, one class code per category
  3. Change analysisGains, losses and transitions between dates
  4. Markov chainTransition probabilities set quantities
  5. Cellular automataSuitability and neighbourhood set locations
  6. CheckSimulate a known year and compare

Two projects compared

Dharan

Built-up land roughly doubles by 2050, mainly on farmland around the existing core. Water bodies shrink by more than a third in the scenario, which deserves a separate check against river-course change and classification differences before it is interpreted.

Rakam Karnali

Built-up land grows by an almost constant 47 ha per decade (about 4.7 ha a year), so percentage growth falls from +74% in the first decade to +23% in the last. Effectively every new hectare of built-up land comes from farmland, along road corridors and a riverside strip.
